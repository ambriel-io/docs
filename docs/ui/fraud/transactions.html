Transactions

The Transactions page is your operational queue for every scored payment, signup, and login: each row shows the final decision, the risk score, and the signals behind it. Use it to triage review-band traffic daily, investigate individual cases with full evidence, and export audit-ready records for compliance.

What the page shows

Each transaction row summarizes the full output of the Scoring Engine in one line: a unique transaction ID, timestamp, customer reference, amount and currency, payment method, the final decision state (Approve, Review, or Reject), and a numeric risk score from 0 to 100. Opening a row reveals the evidence panel — which rules fired, the Digital Footprint signals attached to the session (device, IP, email, phone), and the complete decision history including manual overrides and analyst notes.

Decisions shown here are final: automated rule outcomes plus any manual review actions. When a reviewer changes a state, the original score and rule trace are preserved so audits always show what the engine decided first.

Scores and states update in near real time as new events stream in, so the queue always reflects the latest posture. High-risk spikes — for example a burst of transactions from one device or IP range — surface at the top when you sort by score descending, which is the recommended default view for a morning triage shift.

Filtering transactions

Use filters to narrow the queue to the slice you need to work. Combine several filters at once — for instance, state Review plus score above 70 plus the last 24 hours — to build a focused review list.

Saved views keep your most-used combinations one click away. A typical team keeps three: "Morning review" (Review state, score 50+, last 24h), "High value" (amount above threshold, any state), and "Disputes" (filtered by customer on demand).

Risk-band meanings

Scores map to decision bands that tell you how to act at a glance. Your exact thresholds are configurable in the Scoring Engine, but the default semantics are:

BandScoreMeaningRecommended action
Low risk0–29Signals look clean; no rule fired or only informational rules matchedAuto-approve; sample periodically for QA
Medium risk30–69One or more risk signals present but not conclusiveRoute to manual review or step-up authentication
High risk70–100Strong fraud indicators or a Reject rule matchedBlock or hold; review before any release

Bands are guidance, not verdicts: a low-score transaction with a fresh dispute still deserves a look, and a high-score transaction from a known VIP may warrant a call rather than an outright cancel.

Triaging the review queue

Sort by score, filter to Review

Start each shift with decision state set to Review and sort by score descending. The riskiest cases needing a human decision sit at the top.

Open the evidence panel

Check which rules fired, the score breakdown, and the attached footprint: device reputation, IP geolocation versus billing country, email age and breach exposure, and phone carrier and line type.

Decide: approve, reject, or escalate

Approve genuine buyers, reject clear fraud, and escalate edge cases with a note. Every action records who decided, when, and why, so the next reviewer sees the full trail.

Feed the outcome back into rules

If a pattern repeats — the same BIN range, device, or email domain — take it to the Scoring Engine and tighten or add a rule so the next wave is handled automatically.

Export and audit trail

Select any set of transactions and export them to CSV for chargeback representment, internal reporting, or regulator requests. Each export includes the score, the decision state, the rules that fired, manual actions with analyst identities and timestamps, and the linked footprint evidence. Because the original automated decision is stored separately from later manual overrides, auditors can always distinguish what the engine decided from what a human changed — which is exactly what dispute and compliance reviews require.
